# M18 — The expectation file learns to say what it currently cannot

**Depends on:** [M16](m16.md), hard — it changes expectation rows. [M17](m17.md)
is an *ordering preference*: doing the trees that fit the schema first keeps the
two kinds of gap apart.
**Discharges:** `F5` and `F8` in [findings.md](../findings.md).

Two things a checker does cannot be expressed by `expected.yaml` at all, so two
implementations can differ on both while reproducing every row. `F5`: the audit
**refusing to start** — six conditions are specified, and the file's vocabulary is
the five audit states, none of which means *the run did not happen*. `F8`: a
judgment entry's annotation, which is the one channel that exists to stop a
mechanical run from looking complete, and which the only implementation currently
emits wrongly against this repository's own tree.

## Done means

- **The schema answers `F5`.** A tree whose expectation is that the audit refuses
  to start can be written and pinned. The shape — a third section in
  `expected.yaml`, or a per-tree expectation outside the five states — is decided
  and argued, not defaulted to.
- **The schema answers `F8`.** A judgment entry's annotation can be pinned, so an
  implementation that reports a mapped and readable role as unreadable is caught
  by a tree rather than by a person noticing.
- **`F8`'s underlying defect is separated from its gap.** The annotation is wrong
  on a real tree — `findings` is mapped, `FQ-01` reports `ok` in the same run, and
  the entry still annotates it unresolved. Whether that is the implementation's
  defect, the specification's, or the cross-module `read:` rule's is answered
  before the schema is changed to pin it. Pinning a wrong behaviour is worse than
  pinning nothing.
- **A tree exists for at least one of the six refusal conditions**, so the new
  channel is exercised rather than specified. The remaining five are pinned or
  listed as unpinnable with why, per [M17](m17.md)'s rule.
- The version consequence is stated at the change: this is a contract change and a
  MAJOR for every module whose `expected.yaml` gains a section, and the
  [CHANGELOG.md](../../../CHANGELOG.md) row says what an implementer must re-read.
- `F5` and `F8` move to *Closed* with their dispositions.

## Risks

**This is the unit most likely to grow the specification while claiming to
measure it.** A new section in `expected.yaml` is a new thing every implementer
must parse, and the refusal conditions are the part of the contract where a
checker's behaviour matters most and is least visible. The defence is that all six
conditions are already specified and already required — the change gives them a
tree, it does not add behaviour.

`F8`'s two halves can be confused, and confusing them produces the worst outcome
available: a schema channel built to pin an annotation that the specification
should not have been asking for. The unit's ordering — diagnose, then pin — exists
for that and is the bullet most worth enforcing.

The judgment channel is also the one part of a run a machine cannot settle. A
channel designed to be read by a person, pinned by a file compared by a machine,
is a shape worth being suspicious of; if the resolution is that annotations stay
unpinnable, that is a legitimate outcome and `F8` closes as a stated limit rather
than as a fix.
